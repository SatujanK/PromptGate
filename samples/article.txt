How Students Can Sleep Better During Exam Season

Section 1. Why sleep matters for learning
During sleep, the brain replays what it learned during the day and stores it in long-term memory. Students who sleep fewer than six hours before an exam recall noticeably less than students who sleep seven to nine hours, even when the short sleepers studied longer.

Section 2. Common habits that hurt sleep
Late-night cramming, energy drinks after mid-afternoon, and screens in bed all push sleep later. Irregular wake-up times are the hardest to notice: sleeping in at weekends shifts the body clock, so Monday nights feel like jet lag.

Section 3. A simple plan that works
Pick a fixed wake-up time and keep it every day, including weekends. Stop caffeine six hours before bed. Do the hardest revision in the morning, and keep the last hour before bed for light review away from the desk. If you can't sleep after twenty minutes, get up, read something dull, and go back when you feel sleepy.
